Privacy Policy for CardVault

Last updated: 6 October 2026 · Published by Stratus Flight and Field LLC

The short version

Everything you put into CardVault stays in the app's private storage on your device. The app itself makes no network requests, there is no account to create and no server to sign in to, and the developer never receives your cards or anything else from the app.

There is one exception worth stating plainly. The Google ML Kit component that reads text from a scanned card reports diagnostic and usage data about its own operation to Google. It never sends your cards, your images, or the text read from them. It is described in full under Text recognition and Google ML Kit.

What CardVault stores, and where

Business cards you scan, tap, import or type — names, job titles, companies, phone numbers, email addresses, websites, postal addresses, tags and your own notes — are stored in a database inside the app's private storage on your device. Photographs of cards you scan are stored the same way. The Legacy pages you write are stored the same way.

Apple's standard device backup may include this data if you have iCloud Backup or encrypted local backups turned on. That is a function of iOS, controlled by you in your device settings, and the developer has no access to it.

What leaves your device

None of your cards or their contents, unless you choose to send them.

  • Text recognition of a scanned card runs entirely on your device. No image and no text is uploaded. The ML Kit SDK that performs it does send Google diagnostic data about itself — see below.
  • NFC reads happen on your device.
  • Sharing a card sends a standard .vcf file only to wherever you send it, using the iOS share sheet. CardVault is not a party to it.
  • Backups are files you create and keep. They are not sent anywhere.

Text recognition and Google ML Kit

CardVault reads the text on a scanned card using Google's ML Kit. The recognition runs on your device. The card image, and the text recovered from it, are never sent to Google, to the developer, or to any other server.

Separately from the recognition itself, the ML Kit SDK reports on its own operation to Google. Per Google's disclosure this may include:

  • device information — manufacturer, model, operating system version and build
  • the app's bundle identifier and version
  • a per-installation identifier, which Google states is not intended to uniquely identify a user
  • performance metrics such as processing latency
  • configuration details such as image format and resolution
  • event types such as feature initialisation and detection events
  • error codes arising from the feature's operation

This is information about the software and the device, not about you. No card image, recognised text, contact detail or note is included. It goes to Google, not to the developer, who receives none of it. Google describes it on its ML Kit iOS data disclosure page, and its handling is governed by Google's Privacy Policy. This reporting is part of the SDK; the app offers no setting to turn it off.

Permissions CardVault asks for

  • Camera — to photograph a business card so its text can be read. Images are kept on your device.
  • NFC — to read NFC-enabled business cards you tap.
  • Photo library, where applicable — only to save or select an image you choose.

Purchases

CardVault is unlocked with a one-time in-app purchase processed by Apple. Apple handles the transaction and the payment details; the developer never sees your payment information. CardVault records only whether the unlock has been purchased, stored on your device.

Third parties

CardVault contains no advertising and no tracking, and the developer operates no analytics and receives no usage data about you. Nothing is sold. Two third parties are involved: Apple, which processes the purchase and distributes the app, and Google, whose ML Kit provides on-device text recognition and collects the diagnostic data described above.

Children

CardVault is a business utility and is not directed to children under 13. It collects no personal information from anyone, including children. The ML Kit diagnostic data described above is collected by Google's SDK and concerns the device and the software rather than the person using it.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app deletes its database and images. There is no server-side copy to request deletion of. Keep a backup if you want your cards to survive deleting the app or replacing your phone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the revised version will be posted here with a new date.
